Before each answer

The five checks. Check 0 is the one that cost the most marks this week.

  1. 0Full read. Translate each option into about five plain words. You may not eliminate one until you can point to the words that make it wrong. The deciding qualifier is usually at the end of the option.
  2. 1Stem verb. Objection or challenge means say why it is wrong. Recommend or should means say what to do. Belongs on the list means match the category.
  3. 2Category. Does this option fit the list the stem names, and does it change the choice? A true statement on the wrong list is still wrong.
  4. 3Contradiction. Can both of my picks be true at the same time? Opposites mean one is wrong.
  5. 4Count. Select two means exactly two. On yes/no statements, judge every option on its own.

The eight rules

Must not happen means code, permission or approval.Strength order: code and approvals, then filters and classifiers, then prompt instructions. An instruction is the weakest control, never the answer to a hard requirement.
Route human review by impact, not by confidence.A model can be 0.97 confident and wrong. Ask what happens if this output is wrong.
A control has to act.Logging, alerting and dashboards are monitoring. Enforcement blocks, gates or rejects, and records that it did.
Your own measured numbers beat any vendor or public score.Agreed bar over benchmark rank. Eval on your workload over the vendor's suite. A business case uses measures you can recompute.
Documents go through retrieval. Live state goes through a tool call.Order status, balances, stock and ticket status are read at request time. Re-indexing faster never closes the gap.
Never escalate a fix that already failed.If the stem says they raised the timeout or added retries and it got worse, any option doing more of it is wrong. Resilience is a limit plus a fail-fast detect.
Prefer the change that makes the failure impossible.Gate the tool rather than filter the attack. Fix the ranking rather than widen the window. Enforce the schema rather than repair the output.
Simplest thing that meets the bar.Augmented LLM, then workflow, then agent, then multi-agent. Multi-agent costs roughly 15x the tokens. Do not build for hypothetical future needs.

Evaluation

  • Layer localisation. The fault sits in the layer whose signal moved. Layers whose signals held are cleared. Never blame the judge that did not change, and never relax a signal to quieten it.
  • Pre-register. Primary measure, decision rule, sample and assignment, stopping rule. All fixed before the run. Adding a measure mid-run is cherry-picking.
  • Held-out data. Never quote a score from the set you tuned on or selected on. Small sets mean wide margins. The set must cover every intent and segment you claim for.
  • Judges are instruments. Calibrate against human labels before a judge gates a release.
  • pass@k vs pass^k. Best of k attempts suits a human picking. Unattended single-shot work needs per-attempt reliability.
  • Production monitoring. Sampled scoring of real traffic plus behavioural proxies. A fixed offline set stays green while inputs drift.

  • Incidents: prompt, in business terms, with impact, cause as known, fix, remediation and prevention.

Prompting and models

  • Correct instruction, inconsistent output means few-shot examples. Never a pipeline, never a bigger model.
  • Prompt caching: static content first, byte-identical across requests. Variable content last.
  • Long context: documents at the top, question at the end, quotes extracted first.
  • XML tags separate instructions, documents and examples.
  • Extended thinking for multi-step reasoning where latency is tolerable. Not for high-volume classification or extraction.
  • Leading questions get agreement. Ask for an even-handed comparison against stated criteria.
  • Model choice comes from your eval on your workload, plus cost and latency fit. Not from newest or most capable.
